    Read both numbers together. “Found all tables” can always be pushed to
    100% by simply sending the whole database — so it means nothing without
    “schema sent” beside it. The benchmark databases average 4.5 tables, so there
    is almost nothing to filter out. The bigger the database, the more we save:
    91.5% → 70.6% → 48.7% as size grows 4.5 → 16 → 68 tables.

    Word matching alone finds everything for under a quarter of questions. Meaning
    matching takes it to 91%. Following foreign-key links completes it.
    Capping hub tables cuts the prompt by 15% at identical accuracy — on
    this database 16 tables point at product, so one hop used to drag
    all of them in.

    On the benchmark, the average question already needs more than a third of the
    whole database — there is nothing to filter. On a real 68-table database it
    needs 3.4%, so filtering is the entire job. This is why we stopped
    trusting the benchmark and built our own large-schema evaluation.
  


  

    Honest limits. These measure whether we retrieve the right tables —
    the ceiling on final accuracy, not final accuracy itself. End-to-end answer
    accuracy is the next milestone. Sakila still fails 3 of 20 questions, and we
    know exactly why.
